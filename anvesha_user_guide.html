ANVESHA
AI-assisted Kidney Stone Intelligence & Personal Health Monitoring Platform
User Manual & Feature Guide
1 What is ANVESHA?

ANVESHA is a personal health monitoring and intelligent medical scan platform designed to help patients, family caregivers, and physicians easily track, analyze, and manage kidney health records and Non-Contrast Computed Tomography (NCCT) abdomen CT scans.

Kidney stones (calculi) are a common health concern. ANVESHA makes complex medical scan information easy to understand by providing simple visual summaries, calculus metrics (size, density, location), hydronephrosis grading, and printable doctor report summaries.

2 Key Benefits
💡 For Patients & Families
  • Easy Understanding: No medical degree required. Translates raw CT scans into simple numbers and clear guides.
  • Family Health Profiles: Keep track of multiple family members' health records in one safe place.
  • Printable Doctor Reports: Download or print ready-to-use medical summary reports for physician visits.
  • Safety Alerts: Instant warnings to help identify when emergency medical care is needed.
🩺 For Healthcare Providers
  • Automated Scan Intake: Fast processing of DICOM CT scan volumes (.dcm, .zip, .nii).
  • Calculus Metrics: Calculates maximum stone size (mm), Hounsfield Unit (HU) density, and hydronephrosis grade.
  • Axial Slice Viewer: Interactive slice simulator with Bone/Stone and Soft Tissue contrast presets.
  • Digital Review Sign-off: Save clinical impression notes with secure digital verification signatures.
3 How ANVESHA Works: Step-by-Step Guide

Using ANVESHA is straightforward. Follow these steps to navigate and use all platform features:

Step 1: Personal Health Overview (Home / Dashboard)
Dashboard

When you log in to ANVESHA, you start on the Home Dashboard. Here you can view:

  • Health Profiles: Total family and personal profiles registered.
  • CT Scans Analyzed: Number of completed scan evaluations.
  • Scans Awaiting Review: Scans currently being reviewed by a doctor.
  • High-Risk Calculi Warning Cards: Highlights stones larger than 7.0mm or scans flagged with significant kidney blockage (hydronephrosis).
Step 2: Managing Health Profiles (My Health Records)
Patients Tab

Click on My Health Records in the side menu to view and add profile records:

  • Click "+ Add New Health Profile" to register a family member or personal profile.
  • Enter profile name, age, gender, medical record number (MRN), contact number, and medical history.
  • Filter profile records by risk category (Critical, High, Moderate, Low).
Step 3: Scan Upload & Intake (Scan Upload)
Upload Tab

To analyze a new CT scan file:

  • Click Scan Upload on the sidebar.
  • Drag & drop your CT scan file (accepted formats: .dcm, .zip, .nii, max 500 MB).
  • Click "Start Automated Scan Intake". ANVESHA will validate DICOM metadata headers, spatial resolution, and slice thickness.
  • Alternatively, click "Load Pre-Configured DICOM Sample" to test the features instantly.
Step 4: Interactive Slice Visualizer & Findings (CT Analysis)
Findings Tab

Explore your CT scan interactively:

  • Slice Slider: Scroll slice-by-slice through the axial CT cross-section to view kidney anatomical structures.
  • Contrast Presets: Toggle between Bone/Stone (W:1500 L:400) to highlight hard stones and Soft Tissue (W:400 L:40) to inspect kidney tissue.
  • ROI Bounding Box: Highlight the exact location of detected kidney stones.
  • Calculus Metrics Card: Review key measurements including Stone Location (e.g. Right Lower Pole Calyx), Maximum Size (mm), Density (HU), and Hydronephrosis Grade.
Step 5: Summary Report & Doctor Export (Reports)
Reports Tab

Access the complete printable report:

  • Click "Print Official Summary Report" to generate a clean paper copy or PDF document for your doctor's appointment.
  • Includes clinical impression notes, recommended treatments (e.g., fluid protocols, tamsulosin, urology consultation), and digital sign-off verification.
4 Simple Medical Glossary for Patients

Here is a quick reference table explaining common medical terms used in CT scan reports:

Medical Term What It Means in Plain Language
NCCT Abdomen & Pelvis Non-Contrast Computed Tomography scan. A 3D X-ray scan of the belly taken without injecting dye. It is the gold-standard scan for detecting kidney stones.
Calculus / Calculi The medical term for a kidney stone (or multiple stones).
Hounsfield Units (HU) A measurement of density. Soft tissues are around 30-50 HU, while hard calcium stones are usually 600 to 1200+ HU. Higher density stones are harder to break.
Hydronephrosis Swelling of the kidney caused when a stone blocks the flow of urine down the ureter tube. Graded from Grade 1 (Mild) to Grade 4 (Severe).
Ureteric Dilatation Wideness or swelling of the urine tube connecting the kidney to the bladder due to a downstream stone obstruction.
Medical Expulsive Therapy (MET) Medications (such as Tamsulosin) prescribed by doctors to relax muscles in the ureter and help small stones (< 5-6mm) pass naturally in urine.
⚠️ URGENT MEDICAL SAFETY NOTICE

ANVESHA provides health tracking and educational insights. It is not a replacement for professional medical advice or emergency care.

Seek Emergency Hospital Care Immediately if you experience:
• Severe side or back (flank) pain accompanied by high fever or chills
• Severe nausea, vomiting, or total inability to pass urine
• Visible blood in urine combined with severe fever
